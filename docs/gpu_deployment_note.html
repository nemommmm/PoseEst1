1. Executive summary

The accepted remote backend remains deterministic PyTorch FP32 + YOLOv8m-pose + SKT. It reproduced the accepted historical output exactly in all repeated tests and sustained approximately 30 fps while processing two stored video files.

Important scope: the reported pipeline FPS is offline throughput for two stored files, including decode, pose, tracking, and per-frame geometry. It is not a completed live system with camera capture, display, transport, and downstream output.
  • TensorRT FP32/FP16: deterministic across repeats, but every candidate failed output equivalence and showed no stable end-to-end throughput gain.
  • NVDEC: faster in a decode-only test on a compatible proxy, but that proxy changed the reconstructed pose and was rejected as a formal-input replacement.
  • NVIDIA BodyPose3DNet Accuracy: showed lower right-elbow disagreement on the matched same-input frames, but failed the stereo epipolar gate; it is not admitted.

7. Deployment decision, limitations, and provenance

  1. Keep the accepted baseline: deterministic PyTorch FP32 + YOLOv8m + SKT.
  2. Do not deploy the tested TensorRT engines: median model latency alone did not translate into reliable paired-file speed, and the reconstructed output changed materially.
  3. Treat NVDEC as an input-path engineering task: the valid 1.512× result is decode-only; the tested compatible proxy is not pose-equivalent.
  4. Keep BodyPose3DNet Accuracy as a research lead only: improve cross-view association and epipolar consistency before any far-view, full-data, or deployment claim.
  5. Do not extrapolate A6000 results to RTX 30-series or Jetson hardware: target devices require direct measurement.

Study limitations

  • Repeated deployment timing covers 200-frame windows from two stored stereo sequences, not a long-running live camera system.
  • The NVIDIA feasibility result covers one near-view Fanbo7 recording and near-lossless technical proxies; its stereo geometry did not pass.
  • The Xsens-derived reference has calibration and offset limitations, especially around the elbow. Reported angle values measure system agreement, not absolute truth.
