1. Pipeline × Model Ablation Study

1.1 Experiment design & what V2 actually changed

Amrit had raised a clear request earlier: he wanted to know whether the recent pipeline improvements came from "pipeline changes" or from "YOLO model upgrade". To answer that, I ran a 2×2 ablation matrix:

YOLOv8m (original model)YOLO11l (new model)
V1 pipeline (version from a few weeks ago)V1+YOLOv8mV1+YOLO11l
V2 pipeline (current version)V2+YOLOv8mV2+YOLO11l

What changed from V1 → V2 (recap from last week)

For clarity in later discussion, here is a quick recap of the core V2 improvements over V1:

  • Discarded the old "binary filter + linear interpolation + moving average" chain: in V1, the quality filter was a pass/fail cutoff, and many mid-quality frames were thrown out as NaN. Then linear_fill drew a straight line across the NaN gap, and moving_avg mixed this artificial line with the real values — which literally created large "jumps" that didn't exist in the raw signal at the filter boundaries.
  • Switched to "quality-aware repair + keypoint-level Savitzky-Golay smoothing": keypoints are now sorted into three tiers per frame — valid / repairable / invalid. Mid-quality frames get repaired from neighboring valid frames instead of being discarded. Smoothing was also moved from "angle-domain moving average" to "3D-coordinate-domain Savgol", which is geometrically more consistent and doesn't flatten real motion peaks.
  • Added ROI tracking + weighted DLT + soft epipolar constraint: tracks the target person's bbox across frames to reduce left-right mismatch; weights the triangulation by per-keypoint confidence; softly enforces epipolar geometry to tolerate small calibration errors.
  • Bone-length prior (bone constraint): automatically estimates limb lengths from stable frames (no user height input needed) and applies them as a soft constraint during triangulation, suppressing depth jitter.
  • Optional 2D-model upgrade to YOLO11l — but this experiment specifically evaluates whether that step actually helps.

On the history of the model upgrade: the original decision to switch from YOLOv8m to YOLO11l was made based on fanbo7 under the then-current V1 pipeline + chain post-processing — under that setup YOLO11l dropped the MAE from 6.75° to 2.32° (−65.7%), a very significant improvement. That decision was clearly correct at the time.
Later, V2 pipeline introduced the bone+Savgol+depth post-processing, which automatically compensates for many of YOLOv8m's detection weaknesses — so in near-distance scenarios YOLOv8m can now reach similar results. Meanwhile, new scenarios such as fanbo4 (far) and fanbo9 (dual-camera) exposed a more systematic keypoint bias of YOLO11l on far/small targets, so overall YOLOv8m turns out to be more robust today.

1.3 Why does the "bigger model" perform worse in some scenarios?

Intuitively a larger model should always be more accurate — but in practice YOLO11l clearly regressed on far distance and on fanbo9. Looking at the raw 2D detection quality reveals the mechanism:

Notable anomaly: on fanbo4 far, YOLO11l's confidence is actually higher (0.982 vs 0.976), but its left-right consistency and reprojection accuracy are worse. In other words, at far distance YOLO11l is "confident but not accurate" — and this "over-confidence" is especially harmful in our downstream stereo triangulation, because the pipeline weights inputs by confidence and would treat these wrong estimates as reliable observations.

Three concrete explanations, in plain terms:

  1. At far distance, the person occupies far fewer pixels in the image. At fanbo7 (180 cm) the subject may be 800+ pixels tall in the frame; at fanbo4 (410 cm) only 300-400 pixels. The pixel budget for locating any single joint shrinks by more than half, and any sub-pixel bias gets amplified proportionally.
  2. A larger model is not automatically better at every scale. YOLO11l has ~30% more parameters than YOLOv8m, a larger receptive field, and more abstract features. On the COCO training set, humans are typically 100-500 pixels tall, so at fanbo7 (~500-800 px) — the "normal" range — YOLO11l is noticeably finer than YOLOv8m. But once the target shrinks to fanbo4 range (~300 px), which is under-represented in training data, the larger model tends to rely on its human-body prior to "guess" joint locations, producing systematic biases. YOLOv8m, with its smaller capacity and simpler structure, follows the raw pixel signal more faithfully.
  3. Stereo triangulation is extremely sensitive to left-right consistency. Even if one model produces slightly more accurate 2D keypoints per view, as long as its bias is inconsistent between left and right views (e.g., 3 px too high on the left image, 2 px too low on the right), triangulation gives a "skewed" 3D point. For a stereo system, "a modest model that is consistent across views" is more valuable than "a superior monocular model whose per-view biases differ". The rise of YOLO11l's epipolar p95 from 1.41 to 2.16 on fanbo4 is a direct symptom of this.

This also explains why both fanbo9 cameras (the closer A257 and the farther A255) prefer YOLOv8m — whenever the scenario involves distant subjects or oblique viewpoints, YOLO11l's over-confidence + left-right inconsistency becomes a liability.

Overall conclusion: V2 pipeline + YOLOv8m is the most suitable combination for now.
  • Pipeline upgrade (V1→V2) is positive across all 5 scenarios — up to −52.7% MAE reduction on the far-standing case. The recent progress comes primarily from pipeline improvements (three-tier quality repair, Savgol smoothing, bone constraint, tracking).
  • Model upgrade (YOLOv8m→YOLO11l) helps in near-distance standing (fanbo7 under chain: −20.8%), but becomes unstable at far distance (fanbo4 +57.8%, fanbo9 +11–13%) and on walking data.
  • Advantage of V2+YOLOv8m: doesn't "blow up" in any scenario — even when not the best, it's still "good enough". Combined with its smaller size (faster inference, easier to deploy), this is the most robust choice.

2. fanbo9 Dual-Camera Validation

2.1 Why fanbo9

All previous tests used only one stereo camera (either A255 or A257). fanbo9 is the first case in the dataset where both cameras record the same session simultaneously, which lets us validate whether the same pipeline gives consistent results at two different viewpoints/distances.

Contents: a series of one-degree-of-freedom motions performed one at a time (head fwd/back, head L/R, head rotation, arm side/front lifts, elbow flexion, waist bend/rotate, single-leg raises, ankle rotations...), about 66 seconds total.

The gap is stable across all 4 configs, showing that camera-to-subject distance itself is the dominant factor — the closer the camera, the higher the triangulation depth precision, and the more stable the pose.
Consistent with earlier findings:
  • fanbo4 (far, 410 cm) vs fanbo7 (near, 180 cm) had already shown that distance strongly affects accuracy. fanbo9 now confirms this using the same session across two cameras, ruling out "different motion content" as a confounder.
  • On A257 (closer), V2+YOLOv8m (6.42°) is again the best of the 4 configs — further supporting the conclusion in Section 1.
  • For actual deployment, the subject should stay within ~2-3 m of the camera. Accuracy degrades noticeably past 4 m.

3. FastSAM3D Fusion (original idea turns out infeasible)

An earlier idea was: since FastSAM3D also outputs human pose, could we fuse it into our stereo pipeline, e.g. use its output as an additional observation alongside the YOLO-based stereo triangulation?

This week I carefully read the Fast SAM 3D Body paper (USC / NVIDIA / Meta, 2026) and found that this fusion is technically infeasible. Two main reasons:

3.1 FastSAM3D outputs 3D directly and skips 2D → 3D triangulation

Our stereo pipeline works this way:

Left 2D keypoints  +  right 2D keypoints  +  camera calibration  →  triangulation  →  3D keypoints

FastSAM3D works this way:

single RGB image  →  backbone + Transformer decoding  →  direct 3D mesh + 3D joints

Its depth is inferred by the neural network from a monocular image (using field-of-view estimation and human-body priors), completely bypassing the "cross-view geometry" step. So FastSAM3D doesn't produce an intermediate variable that can be triangulated with another view — it goes directly to 3D in one shot.

3.2 Extracting accurate 2D keypoints from inside FastSAM3D is also not viable

Internally, FastSAM3D does use a YOLO11-Pose detector, but the paper explicitly states this YOLO's role is only to give a coarse bounding box so the downstream Transformer knows where the person is (paper's own words: "these initial keypoints do not require sub-pixel precision; they merely need to establish a reliable bounding region").

The real pose precision comes from the Transformer decoder afterward, and this precision only surfaces in the 3D mesh output — never as accurate 2D keypoints. Even if we opened up FastSAM3D's pipeline, we couldn't extract "high-precision 2D keypoints" to feed into stereo triangulation; we could only extract the coarse 2D from YOLO11-Pose, which we already use in our own pipeline — no additional value.

Conclusion: FastSAM3D is not suitable as an "additional 2D observation" for the stereo system.
Correct usage: treat FastSAM3D as an independent reference (which is exactly how we already use it today) — its output and our stereo output stand alone, and we compare MAE against Xsens separately. Any "fusion" can only happen at the result level (e.g., "switch to FastSAM3D when the stereo pipeline's confidence is low"), not at the geometric level.
